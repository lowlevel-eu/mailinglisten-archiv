> From: Max Reitz <max at tyndur.org>

> This patch is a proposal for a CDI.audio interface, still without the
> mixer part (which either may be introduced later as a new CDI driver
> interface or as an OS unspecific library).
> 
> I hope the header to be well commented, thus I don't have to explain
> every detail but I think it can't hurt to give you an overview of it:
> 
> Every driver provides several devices (of course), each device is
> either capable of receiving (recording) or sending (playing) data.
> Furthermore, every device is divided into one or more subdevices, which
> are called streams (if anyone knows a better name, I'd appreciate it).

The German comment says "Kanal", so what about "channel"?

> The actual playback or recording is done via those streams, the system
> sends samples to them and they are then mixed by the device. Thus,
> recording devices provide just one stream, because mixing makes no
> sense.

> Max

Hm, shouldn't that be a property of a stream/channel? It seems odd to
have two devices appearing in the system for one physical sound card,
just to have one for playback and one for recording. It's like having
a different devices for reading from a disk than for writing to it.

Would it make sense to use a cdi_mem_area instead of size_t/void*?

How is success/error defined? You say "The amount of data transmitted
equals the buffer's size" (I assume this refers to stream->buffer_size).
So is buffer != stream->buffer_size an error or would it result in
something like a short write?

Also, what's the state after a transfer_data() call? I suppose no data
has been queued in that case?

Is there a way to update the data later? I'm thinking of a mixer that
sent 30 seconds in advance to keep the CPU load low, but then the user
hit the stop button. If you completely stop the audio device and restart
it, other sounds being played at the same time would stop for some time,
too. Sounds easy enough to add such a function later, though, so feel
free to ignore my thoughts on this for now.
